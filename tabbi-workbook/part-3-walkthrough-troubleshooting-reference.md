## TROUBLESHOOTING

### SPREAD 1 (LEFT PAGE): I'm Not Actually Using This

**The problem:** You set it up. You filled in the dashboard. And then you didn't actually use it. It's just sitting there. You're back to holding everything in your head.

**The real problem:** The barrier to using it is bigger than the benefit. It's too complicated. It takes too long to open. It doesn't look right to you. Something about it feels wrong.

**How to fix it:**

1. What's the actual barrier? (Is it opening it? Understanding it? The way it looks? The categories not matching your brain?)
2. Fix that one thing.
3. Try again.

The system has to be easier than holding everything in your head. If it's not, you won't use it.

Maybe it's too much. Start with just one tab. Track just the urgent stuff. Add complexity later.

Maybe it looks wrong. Change the colors. Make the text bigger. Tear out pages and stick them on your wall.

Maybe it takes too long. Simplify. You don't need every feature. You need the features that actually help you not lose stuff.

---

### SPREAD 1 (RIGHT PAGE): What Breaks What You Don't Use

**Common reasons people stop using Tabbi:**

- Too many tabs (reduce to 3, seriously)
- Too many recurring reminders (cut it in half)
- The visual system doesn't match how your brain works (change it)
- You have to open too many things to see your actual day (combine stuff)
- Your anchor got sacrificed and you're exhausted (protect it again)
- You tried to be perfect instead of just tracking what's actually happening (stop)

Pick the one that's true for you. Fix it. Try again.

### SPREAD 2 (LEFT PAGE): Everything's Due at Once and I'm Underwater

**The problem:** You have a deadline. Or three. Everything is urgent. Your system is screaming. You can't breathe.

**This is not a system failure.** This is a life event. Systems don't protect you from real deadlines. They just help you see them coming.

**How to handle it:**

1. Your anchor is protected (non-negotiable). You need it now more than ever.
2. Everything else is triage. What's actually due first?
3. Use your status badges. Mark everything red or urgent or whatever your signal is.
4. Focus only on red. Nothing else matters until that's done.
5. When one deadline passes, move to the next red thing.
6. Sleep. Eat. Protect your anchor. Then keep going.

You survive crunch by having an anchor. Without it, crunch is catastrophic.

### SPREAD 3 (LEFT PAGE): My Anchor Keeps Getting Interrupted

**The problem:** You scheduled your anchor. You said it was non-negotiable. And then something came up and it got moved. And moved again. And now you haven't had your anchor time in weeks.

**This happens.** Especially to caregivers and parents. Or people whose jobs have constant demands.

**How to fix it:**

1. Is your anchor too specific? (If "6pm walk" keeps getting interrupted, maybe "sometime between 5-8pm" works better.)
2. Do people know your anchor is protected? (Tell them. "This is my time. I need you not to ask for things right now.")
3. Do you actually believe it's non-negotiable, or are you just saying that? (Because if you don't actually believe it, nobody else will either.)
4. What's one version of this that's harder to interrupt? (Earlier? With someone else? Different location?)

Your anchor is the most important thing. If it keeps disappearing, you need a version that's harder to lose.

### SPREAD 4 (LEFT PAGE): My System Broke and I Don't Know Why

**The problem:** It was working. And then something changed and now it's not. You're not using it. You don't know what went wrong.

**Troubleshoot:**

1. What changed? (Your life? The system? Your brain?)
2. Did your anchor disappear? (If yes, that's the problem. Fix that first.)
3. Do your tabs still make sense? (If no, rebuild them.)
4. Is there too much stuff? (Delete the recurring reminders you're not actually doing.)
5. Is it too complicated? (Simplify.)

Systems break because life changes. Rebuild it for your new life.

## REFERENCE SECTIONS

### SPREAD 1 (LEFT PAGE): The 7 Elements at a Glance

**Element 1: Tabs**
The big categories in your life. Work, Personal, Money, whatever makes sense for you. You use tabs to sort tasks.

**Element 2: Quick Capture**
Anything that comes into your brain gets written down immediately, right where you are. Your bedside notepad. Your voice memo. A quick screenshot. Anywhere. You sort it later.

**Element 3: Due Dates**
When is this actually due? Write it down. Not "sometime this week." The actual date. Your brain needs that specificity.

**Element 4: Recurring Reminders**
What needs to happen over and over? Medication. Invoicing. Checking in with someone. Weekly team standup. Write it once. Let it remind you.

**Element 5: Status Badges**
What state is this in? Red (urgent, today). Yellow (coming up). Green (handled). Or emoji. Or whatever shows you the visual priority.

**Element 6: Urgency Filtering**
When everything looks urgent, nothing is. You filter to see only today's actual urgent stuff. Then you do those. Then you look at what's coming up. Then everything else.

**Element 7: Visual Customization**
Your system is ugly if you hate it. Make it beautiful to you. Color. Layout. Typography. Make it something you want to use.

---

### SPREAD 1 (RIGHT PAGE): Status Badges Quick Guide

**Visual systems:**

Red / Yellow / Green (color-coded priority)

Emoji (⚠️ = urgent, 📋 = upcoming, ✓ = done)

Text (TODAY / WEEK / LATER / DONE)

Symbols (! vs ~ vs -)

Pick one that your brain reads instantly. Not what's "best." What works for you.

**Recurring Status Badges:**

- TODAY (do this today)
- WEEK (do this this week)
- MONTH (do this this month)
- WAITING (someone else is moving it)
- BLOCKED (can't move forward)
- DONE (already handled)

Use these, or build your own. But keep it simple. If you have too many statuses, you'll stop using them.

### SPREAD 2 (RIGHT PAGE): Urgency Filtering Examples

**When everything is urgent:**

1. Filter to only TODAY (actual deadlines today, not "soon")
2. Do those.
3. Show WEEK (what's coming up this week)
4. Plan for those.
5. Everything else is LATER.

**For different life situations:**

**Employed:** Meetings are TODAY. Deliverables are WEEK. Projects are MONTH.

**Freelancer:** Client work is TODAY. Admin is WEEK. Business development is MONTH.

**Student:** Exams are WEEK. Problem sets are WEEK. Reading is ONGOING.

**Parent:** Kid stuff is TODAY. Appointments are WEEK. Annual stuff is MONTH.

**Caregiver:** Medical stuff is TODAY. Admin is WEEK. You are ALWAYS.

**In Transition:** Applications are WEEK. Interviews are WEEK. Job search is WEEK.

Adjust these for your life.

### SPREAD 3 (RIGHT PAGE): When to Adjust

**Adjust immediately if:**
- Your anchor is disappearing (protect it)
- You stopped using it (too complicated or broken)
- A tab isn't making sense (rename or delete it)
- A reminder isn't helping (delete it)

**Adjust weekly if:**
- You notice patterns (same stuff keeps being missed)
- Your life changed (new job, new kid, new situation)
- Something feels off but you can't name it (try something different)

**Adjust monthly if:**
- You want to try something new (different status system, different tabs)
- You're getting bored (customize it, make it new)
- A bigger life change happened (reference your profile, rebuild)

Systems are not static. You rebuild them as you go. That's not failure. That's how they stay useful.
