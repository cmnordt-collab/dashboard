## SPREADS 7-8: ELEMENT 1 - TABS

### SPREAD 7 (LEFT PAGE): Tabs (Organize Your Life Into Domains)

**Headline:** "One Tab Per Area That Matters"

**Body text:**
- Explain that tabs are just categories/domains
- Your life is not one thing; it's multiple things happening at once
- Each tab is a separate space so things don't bleed into each other
- Examples: Work, Finance, Home, Health, Goals, Relationships, Creative, etc.
- You pick what matters; nobody else decides
- Can start with 3 tabs, add more later, delete ones that don't help
- No "required" tabs

**Key teaching point:** Tabs are not a hierarchy of importance. They're separation for sanity.

## SPREADS 9-10: ELEMENT 2 - QUICK CAPTURE

### SPREAD 9 (LEFT PAGE): Quick Capture (The Brain Dump Bucket)

**Headline:** "Your Thought Doesn't Have to Be Organized to Count"

**Body text:**
- Quick Capture is where EVERYTHING goes before it gets sorted
- It's messy. It's supposed to be.
- You're not deciding what goes where; you're just getting it out
- Can be a inbox in the app, a page in this workbook, voice notes, texts to yourself, whatever
- The rule: if you think of something and don't write it down, it's gone
- Transfer happens when you have energy (could be 10 minutes later, could be 3 weeks)
- No guilt about the lag time
- This is where the Sam insight applies: brain is for ideas, not storage

**Key teaching point:** Speed matters more than accuracy here.

## SPREADS 11-12: ELEMENT 3 - DUE DATES

### SPREAD 11 (LEFT PAGE): Due Dates (Know Your Deadlines)

**Headline:** "So You Don't Get Blindsided"

**Body text:**
- Due dates are visibility, not judgment
- The problem: you don't realize something is due until it's too late
- Due Date Fields show you what's actually due and when
- Lead-time alerts: you set when you want a heads-up (1 week before? 3 days? 1 hour?)
- Without lead time, you get surprised. With it, you can plan
- Different things have different lead times (presentation prep needs more notice than "wash dishes")
- You decide the lead time for each category

**Key teaching point:** Deadlines aren't scary if you see them coming.

## SPREADS 13-14: ELEMENT 4 - RECURRING REMINDERS

### SPREAD 13 (LEFT PAGE): Recurring Reminders (The Things That Repeat)

**Headline:** "No Streaks. No Shame. Just Reset."

**Body text:**
- Some things repeat: take medication, pay rent, water plants, call your mom, backup your computer
- Recurring Reminders auto-reset so you don't have to remember to remember
- No streaks, no guilt if you miss one
- Visual emoji or icon so you scan instead of read
- You pick the frequency (daily, weekly, monthly) and you can change it
- When you complete it, it resets for next time
- This is NOT about building habits or motivation; it's about not forgetting things that matter

**Key teaching point:** You're not trying to change yourself. You're just not forgetting.

## SPREADS 17-20: ELEMENT 6 - URGENCY FILTERING (4 PAGES - MULTIPLE LIFE SCENARIOS)

### SPREAD 17 (LEFT PAGE): Urgency Filtering - The Concept

**Headline:** "Can You Do This Right Now?"

**Body text:**
- Time blindness isn't just "when is it due"
- It's "can I do this at the exact moment I want to"
- Three types:
  - TIME-LOCKED (meetings, calls, appointments — must happen at specific hours)
  - ANYTIME (email, writing, notes, chores — can happen whenever)
  - BLOCKED (waiting for someone/something — can't do yet)
- When your brain is chaotic, this separation saves you
- Reduces "why did I think I could do three things at once" stress
- Visual marker for each type (red/yellow/green or emoji)

**Key teaching point:** Knowing WHAT KIND of task it is helps you decide WHEN to do it.

---

### SPREAD 18 (LEFT PAGE): Urgency Filtering - 9-5 Work Schedule

**Scenario:** "You have a predictable 9-5 job with meetings and focus time"

**Example breakdown:**
- TIME-LOCKED: Status meetings (10am), 1:1 with manager (2pm), client call (3:30pm)
- ANYTIME: Email, report writing, Slack messages, tasks between meetings
- BLOCKED: Waiting for designer to send mockup, waiting for budget approval

**How this helps:** You know your "flow time" is 10:30-12 and 1-2pm. Protect those for ANYTIME work. Avoid scheduling deep focus during BLOCKED stuff.

**Body text:** Teaching content about how this works for someone with a stable schedule

---

### SPREAD 19 (LEFT PAGE): Urgency Filtering - Freelance/Variable Hours

**Scenario:** "You set your own hours, clients call at random, projects overlap"

**Example breakdown:**
- TIME-LOCKED: Client calls (whenever they call), deadline submissions, meeting with collaborators
- ANYTIME: Design work, writing, admin, proposals
- BLOCKED: Waiting for client feedback, waiting for payment to clear, waiting for contractor

**How this helps:** You can't predict your day. Knowing which tasks NEED a specific time slot vs which can flex means you spend less energy on "what can I do right now?"

**Body text:** Teaching content specific to variable schedules

## SPREADS 21-22: ELEMENT 7 - VISUAL CUSTOMIZATION

### SPREAD 21 (LEFT PAGE): Make It Yours (Aesthetics Aren't Extra)

**Headline:** "If You Hate How It Looks, You Won't Open It"

**Body text:**
- Visual customization is not decoration
- You're more likely to use something that feels good to look at
- This is dopamine mining: part of staying engaged
- Choices: color palette, emoji density, text vs visual, typography, layout
- Can be minimal or chaotic; your preference decides
- Can change it whenever the current setup stops working
- Aesthetic serves function: it keeps you coming back

**Key teaching point:** The system you actually use beats the perfect system you don't.
